## The Final Turn

_a specimen source text, descended from SS-0003_

The last weaver wrote one letter and left it folded on the frame. It read, in its entirety:
_Here is my work, which is not finished, and here is my reason, which is that finishing it
would end it._

Those who found the letter disagreed about the tone. Some heard apology. Some heard
instruction. One, much later, argued that the two readings were the same, and that the house
had spent four generations proving it — that an apology for not concluding and an instruction
not to conclude are, in a craft like this one, a single sentence read from either end.

The frame was never cut down. It stands where it stood, with the warp held tight and waiting,
and the letter has been copied so many times that no one now knows which is the original, or
whether the original still exists, or whether it matters. The phrase travels anyway. It arrives
in other texts without announcement, as though it had always been there.
