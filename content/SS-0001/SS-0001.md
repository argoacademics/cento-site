## The Unfinished Hem

_a specimen source text, descended from SS-0003_

There was once a room above a wash-house where a frame stood with the warp strung and the weft
not begun. Those who inherited it disagreed about what it asked of them. One said it was a
question. One said it was an accusation. The youngest said it was neither, only a loom, and
that a remedial solution to their argument would be to simply start weaving.

They did not start. Instead they argued for a season about fidelity — whether to continue in
the hand that began it, or to admit their own and let the join show. To match exactly, said the
eldest, produces a hamstrung hidebound imitation, faithful to the letter and empty of the
reason. To ignore the beginning entirely, said the youngest, is not inheritance at all but
merely reuse.

What they settled on was the seam: to let the change of hand be visible, marked, dated. A
reader running a thumb along the cloth would feel exactly where one weaver stopped and the next
took up. This is the posture the house passed down, and the cloth remembers the hand precisely
because the hand was never hidden.

The work remains unfinished. That is not neglect. It is the form the inheritance takes — each
generation adding a length and stopping deliberately short, so that there is always something
left for the next pair of hands to argue about.
